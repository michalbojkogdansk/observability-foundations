📐 Sturges' Rule
Formula: k = ⌈log₂(n) + 1⌉
Only needs your sample size n. Fast and simple. Works well for small, symmetric (bell-shaped) datasets. Tends to under-bin large datasets and misses fine detail in bimodal or skewed distributions.
e.g. n=500 → ⌈9.0 + 1⌉ = 10 bins
📏 Freedman-Diaconis Rule
Formula: h = 2 × IQR × n−1/3, bins = range / h
Uses the interquartile range (IQR) — the gap between the 25th and 75th percentile — to set bin width. Robust to outliers. But on heavy-tailed data (small IQR, large range) it can suggest thousands of bins — more than any chart can render. That's the signal: your data has extreme spread.
⚠ "Beyond slider" means FD detected a very wide range relative to your IQR. Try the Payments preset to see this in action.
Neither rule is universally correct. They are starting points — your job is to choose the bin count that best reveals the shape you care about for your audience.

Scenario 2

Percentile vs Average Showdown

Start a live request stream. Then inject slow outliers and watch how mean stays calm while p99 screams. See which metric your alert fires on first.

Traffic pattern
📊 Normal — Most requests cluster around a single average with equal tails. A well-tuned internal API with consistent load. Predictable and stable — rare in practice.
📈 Right-skewed — Most requests are fast, but a long tail of slow outliers drags the mean upward. Classic cache-miss pattern: 80% return in 50ms, 5% wait for DB → 2s+. The most common real-world shape. Mean is misleading here — p99 tells the real story.
🔔 Bimodal — Two distinct clusters with a valley between them. Signals two hidden populations: e.g. authenticated (fast, in-memory) vs unauthenticated (slow, DB lookup). A single mean falls in the valley and describes neither group. Split and measure separately.

🟡 Default Prometheus
11 fixed boundaries: 5 10 25 50 100 250 500 1000 2500 5000 10000 ms
Built into Prometheus for general HTTP latency. The chart clips to your data's range, so you'll typically see 8–9 lines. Wide 250→500ms gap causes large SLO interpolation errors. Most teams never change this.
🟣 Coarse
5 boundaries only: 100 500 1000 5000 10000 ms
Minimal cardinality cost — good for high-throughput background jobs where you only need rough visibility. Expect p95/p99 errors over 100ms.
🟢 Tuned
9 boundaries clustered near your SLO: 50 100 150 200 300 500 750 1000 2000 ms
Dense around the threshold that matters most. Best interpolation accuracy for SLOs — but requires knowing your latency profile first.
🔵 Custom
Use the boundary count slider (3–15) to auto-generate log-spaced boundaries across your data range, then fine-tune individual values. See directly how placement shifts the interpolation error % and SLO verdict.
💡 Rule of thumb: place at least one boundary within 10% on each side of your SLO target to keep interpolation error below 5%.
