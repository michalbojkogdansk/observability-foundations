Scenario 1 — TimescaleDB Spike Detector

time_bucket() · z-score anomalies · hypertable scans

Z-score measures how many standard deviations a reading sits above or below its own average — in business terms, a z-score above 2.5 means "this spike is so unusual it would happen by chance less than 1% of the time, treat it as an incident."

TimescaleDB partitions your metrics into hourly hypertables and runs z-score anomaly detection in a single SQL query — far faster than a plain Postgres table scan. Think of it as a specialised edition of PostgreSQL built from the ground up for large volumes of time-stamped data: it speaks standard SQL but adds automatic partitioning by time, so queries over millions of rows stay fast even on a free-tier database.

View SQL queries

Plain Postgres:

SELECT time_bucket('1 hour', ts) AS bucket,
  AVG(value) AS avg_val,
  COUNT(*) AS cnt,
  STDDEV(value) AS std_dev
FROM metrics
WHERE service = $1 AND ts > NOW() - INTERVAL '$2 hours'
GROUP BY bucket ORDER BY bucket;

TimescaleDB (hypertable with chunk exclusion):

SELECT time_bucket('1 hour', ts) AS bucket,
  AVG(value) AS avg_val,
  PERCENTILE_CONT(0.99) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY value) AS p99,
  COUNT(*) AS cnt,
  STDDEV(value) AS std_dev,
  (AVG(value) - AVG(AVG(value)) OVER ())
    / NULLIF(STDDEV(AVG(value)) OVER (), 0) AS z_score
FROM metrics
WHERE service = $1 AND ts > NOW() - INTERVAL '$2 hours'
GROUP BY bucket ORDER BY bucket;
